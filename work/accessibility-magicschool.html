Building accessibility

from the ground up, at MagicSchool

Project Overview

I joined MagicSchool in January 2025 as the company's first dedicated accessibility hire. MagicSchool is an AI-driven ed-tech platform used by millions of educators and students, and when I started, there was no formal accessibility practice at all — no audit, no VPAT, no dedicated a11y expertise on the team. In under two years, I built that practice from scratch: strategy, culture, tooling, process, and compliance.

My Contributions

Some of the bigger pieces of that work:

  • Founded and run a weekly, cross-functional Accessibility Guild spanning Design, Engineering, and Product.
  • Led a full manual accessibility audit and remediated 80 of the 100 issues it surfaced, then published MagicSchool's first public VPAT/ACR.
  • Designed a WCAG-forward design system with accessible components — correct contrast, ARIA, and focus states built in by default.
  • Ran 23+ cross-functional accessibility reviews covering nearly every major feature area.
  • Built an internal accessibility education program and a checklist used across Design, Engineering, and PM.
Why it mattered

The stakes were real, not theoretical.

The world moved fast right as I joined — legally, competitively, and for the people we serve.

Legal urgency

The DOJ updated ADA Title II in April 2024 — every ed-tech vendor selling to public schools now has to meet WCAG 2.1 AA, with deadlines in April 2026 for large districts and April 2027 for small ones.

Business risk

In May 2025, Baltimore City issued a formal Notice of Failure to Meet Standards against MagicSchool. Sales and support were fielding questions they couldn't answer, and partners were running their own audits.

Mission alignment

More than 1 in 4 U.S. adults has a disability. For a company serving educators and students, inaccessibility means exclusion — a direct contradiction of what we're supposed to be doing.

My approach

"Accessibility is not 'on' or 'off.' It is more or less. A product is more or less accessible."— Anna Cook, Microsoft

Progress over perfection

There's no "done" in accessibility. I set up systems to improve continuously rather than chase a one-time certificate.

Shared, cross-functional ownership

Accessibility can't live in one role. I embedded it across Design, Engineering, and Product, so every team is accountable for it.

Shift left

A11y issues are cheap to fix early and expensive to fix later, so I moved checks into the design and ticket phase, not just QA.

Conformance, not just compliance

We build accessible products because it's right, not only because the law requires it. Both happen to be true here.

The audit + VPAT

Full audit. Real remediation.

I contracted accessiBe for a comprehensive manual audit of the full web app, run from May 27 to July 1, 2025. It covered color contrast, keyboard navigation and focus order, screen reader compatibility, form fields and labels, zoom and reflow at 400%, heading hierarchy, image alt text, and interactive elements.

Of the 100 issues it surfaced, we fixed 80 internally — the remaining 20 were in third-party tools outside our control. That gave us an honest picture of where we stood, which fed directly into MagicSchool's first VPAT/ACR, now published publicly at magicschool.ai/accessibility.

Culture + process

The A11y Guild: making it everyone's job.

What the Guild does

  • Weekly cross-functional meetings across Design, Engineering, and PM
  • An accessibility review for every new feature before it ships
  • Squads own the resulting a11y tickets in their own sprint work
  • Internal learning sessions on key WCAG topics
  • Automated accessibility scanning on every pull request

What it's produced

  • An A11y Checklist used across PM, Design, and Engineering
  • 8+ "A11y Bites" learning modules
  • A documented review process and prep guide
  • Automated axe testing running on every PR
  • MagicSchool's 2026 accessibility roadmap
Systems + tooling

Baking accessibility into how we ship.

The goal was to make the accessible path the easy path — so it holds up even when I'm not in the room.

WCAG-forward design system

Built and launched a design system with accessibility in every component — correct contrast ratios, ARIA attributes, focus states, and keyboard patterns by default. Written once, reused everywhere.

Automated testing

Automated axe accessibility scanning runs on every pull request, with a dashboard that tracks trends over time so regressions get caught before they reach users.

A11y checklist

An actionable checklist covering interactive elements, color, keyboard and focus, headings, images, forms, zoom, and screen readers — usable by PMs, designers, and engineers alike.

accessiBe overlay, honestly framed

Kept as a supplemental tool sitting on top of an already-accessible product, not a replacement for real fixes — with clear internal messaging about its limits.

What's next

The work continues.

Testing with disabled users

Moving beyond automated scans to testing with real users of assistive technology — automation can claim progress while the real experience is still broken.

A11y requirements in tickets

Formalizing accessibility requirements at the ticket-writing stage, shifting the work even further left.

A regular review cadence

Moving from one-off requests to a predictable, documented review schedule tied to the product development process.

A customer feedback loop

Building a direct channel for teachers, students, and districts to surface accessibility concerns.

A refreshed accessibility statement

Updating our public statement to accurately reflect where we stand and where we're headed.

AI + accessibility, cautiously

Exploring AI-assisted workflows like drafting alt text, always with a human verifying the result.

"It's okay to not know. But it's not okay to never try."

Accessibility at MagicSchool isn't finished, and it never fully will be. But it's a practice now, not a project — a community and a set of systems that make accessibility the default, not the exception.
